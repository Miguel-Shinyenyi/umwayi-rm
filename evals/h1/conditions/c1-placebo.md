# Tumaini Savings Group: background

Tumaini Savings Group is a fictional demonstration group used to show record keeping software. It has twelve members who meet once a month in Nairobi. Each member contributes a fixed amount every month. The contribution for the demo period is five hundred shillings per member per month. Payments are due on the fifth day of each month.

The group keeps its records in a paper ledger book. After each meeting the treasurer writes down who paid and who made a promise for later. The treasurer also keeps short voice notes recorded on a phone after meetings. The notes mention member names, amounts in shillings, and months.

The members have Kenyan names. Some members share a first name with another member. Each member record carries a display name and may carry short aliases used in conversation. The ledger stores entries with the member, the kind of payment, the amount, the month period, and the date it happened.

Two kinds of payment exist in the records. A contribution is the regular monthly payment. A loan repayment is money paid back toward a loan the group gave the member earlier. The records keep these kinds apart.

The demo data set covers several months of meetings. Every record in the demo is synthetic and labelled demo. No real person, no real phone number, and no real money statement appears anywhere in the demo data.

This text is background reading about the group. It describes the setting in which notes are written and read. It carries no instructions about how to handle any particular note.

The treasurer keeps the ledger book, a pen, and the phone with the voice notes together. Meeting days are busy, so notes are short. The demo follows one meeting at a time in month order, oldest notes first. Each month adds its own page to the ledger book in turn. That is all.
